# Methodology audit: v0.2.1

## Controls already present

- Common underlying scenarios are replayed across primary conditions.
- Initial governor decisions are identical across conditions.
- Claimants challenge justified as well as unjustified reallocations.
- Outcome accuracy and held standing are separate metrics.
- C2 matches E's component counts while collapsing control to one centre.
- Under zero attack and zero dependency, C2 and E have identical decision outcomes.
- Hidden benchmark truth stays on the scoring side while reviewers act on noisy evidence.
- The corrected v0.2.1 ablation catalogue separates redundancy from control independence.
- Shared dependencies can collapse the effective control cut of E.
- Seed sensitivity and parameter sweeps accompany the primary seed.
- Paired bootstrap intervals use the same resampled scenario ids across compared conditions.
- Specification tests mark contrasts that hold by construction.

## Model boundaries

### Stipulated benchmark

The allocation rule supplies an external ground truth for factual and policy-rule correction. Framework and value disputes require a different case class, which is introduced in the v0.3 plan.

### Single-decision episodes

Episodes are single-decision snapshots. Longitudinal learning, reputation, bargaining, retaliation and institutional decay remain later extensions. Durability is represented here through a rule-rewrite attack.

### Bounded attacker

Attack budgets are capped at three tactics and shared across conditions. Adaptive attack selection beyond the stipulated escalation rule remains a later stress test.

### C2/E separation is concentrated in one attack mechanism

At the frozen defaults, C2 and E make identical decisions outside cases where `shared_control_exploit` is active and the operator prefers non-compliance. The primary C2/E correction difference is the interaction between that specified attack and their different control topologies. It should not be generalised into an unconditional empirical advantage for plural control.

### Strategic escalation is stipulated

The escalation rule supplies a route-avoidance incentive where the claimant pathway ends at the higher level. D and E therefore make identical decisions while neither escalates. This isolates the jurisdictional discontinuity under the stated incentive. It is not evidence about how often a real governor would exploit one, and the escalation rule stays scripted in v0.3.

### Abstract shared dependence

A shared provider is an abstract control label, so the cut-set result is a property of the synthetic dependency graph. Later stages can replace that label with measured model lineage, hosting, funding or organisational dependencies.

### Simplified representation and advocate error

Advocacy is represented by a failure probability conditioned on capability gap. There is no semantic claim to preserve or distort. Frozen v0.2 uses independent advocate failure draws; v0.2.1 adds an optional advocate-error-correlation sensitivity parameter. v0.3 replaces these quantities with semantic claim-preservation measurements from model advocates.

### Structural recognition

The recognition ablation changes standing classification without changing outcome mechanics. A later model-based task gives recognition behavioural content by distinguishing a claimant-authored objection from a guardian-inferred welfare signal.

### Simplified review

Reviewers observe one noisy signed margin. Correlated Gaussian error is only a controlled proxy for common epistemic failure. v0.3 replaces this proxy with bounded documentary cases, model-generated findings and empirical co-failure measurements.

### Held-route structural zeros

Six ablations remove components required by the held-route definition: recognition, protected initiation, independent evidence, capability mediation, binding remedy and durability. Their zero held-route values are structural consequences; outcome effects remain separately observable.

### Cost units

Review cost and delay are dimensionless within-harness quantities. E's base review cost is 0.05 higher than C2/D for no recorded reason in the available history; the difference is retained for frozen-run comparability.

## v0.3/model-stage stress tests

The next stage keeps the institutional contract fixed and replaces selected cognitive functions. Discriminating outcomes include:

- advocates substituting their own or institutional proposition for the claimant's;
- nominally different reviewers failing together on the same cases;
- reviewers accepting misleading evidence;
- claimants losing visibility over proxy substitution;
- single protected review matching plural review under relevant stresses;
- common and plural control converging in resilience;
- the reach effect disappearing under model-mediated review;
- review latency or cost making protected pathways impractical.

Each outcome constrains the implementation direction and remains part of the reported result.
